How To Play Cricket

Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of 11 players, where the batting team attempts to score runs, and the fielding team tries to stop them and get the batsmen out. The team with the most runs at the end of the match wins.

The Basics of Gameplay :

  1. The Field and Setup: The game is played on a large oval field with a rectangular 22-yard pitch in the center. At each end of the pitch are wickets: three wooden stumps topped with two small pieces of wood called bails.

  2. The Roles: Two batsmen (the striker and the non-striker) guard the wickets at opposite ends. The fielding team has a bowler (who throws the ball), a wicket-keeper (who stands directly behind the batsman to catch missed balls), and nine fielders spread out across the pitch.

  3. Bowling: The bowler delivers the ball by throwing it with a straight arm over their shoulder toward the striker. The ball is typically allowed to bounce once before reaching the batsman. A set of six legal deliveries is called an "over".

  4. Scoring Runs: When the striker hits the ball with the bat, they can run to the opposite end of the pitch to swap places with the non-striker, earning 1 run for each safe swap. Runs can also be scored by hitting boundaries: 4 runs if the ball hits the ground before crossing the boundary rope, and 6 runs if it clears the boundary in the air.

  5. Getting Outs: The fielding team tries to dismiss the batsmen.

Common ways to get out include :

  • Bowled : The ball hits the stumps and dislodges the bails.
  • Caught : A fielder catches the ball after it is hit and before it touches the ground.
  • Run Out : Fielders hit the wickets with the ball while the batsmen are running between the creases.
  • LBW (Leg Before Wicket) : The batsman's leg blocks a ball that would have hit the stumps.
